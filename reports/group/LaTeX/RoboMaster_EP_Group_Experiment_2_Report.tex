\section{Experiment Scope and Team Organization}
\subsection{Objective and equipment adaptation}
The task is fixed-point pick-and-place without visual localization: return home,
approach point A, descend, grasp, lift, move to point B, release and return home.
The required acceptance target is at least four successful transfers in five
consecutive trials, together with collision, joint-limit and failure checks.

Because the originally specified standalone mechArm 270 equipment was unavailable,
the group used the arm and gripper mounted on a DJI RoboMaster EP chassis. The
simulation uses ROS 2 Humble, Gazebo Fortress and custom kinematics/control; the
physical test uses the RoboMaster Python SDK. This report treats that as a
platform adaptation, not as proof of an unchanged ROS 2 hardware backend.

Xue and Bao jointly performed physical setup, parameter review and integrated

to a passive observer. All five members share the final project outcome.

\subsection{Outcome summary}
The recorded simulation completed five placements and verified home after all
five cycles. The team confirmed five successful hardware transfers on 12
September 2026 with no observed abnormality. Numerical accuracy is available for
the simulation only; the hardware result is observational.

\evidence{The group did not supply measured hardware position error, force,
payload mass or per-cycle duration. Those quantities are not inferred from a 5/5
observed outcome.}

\subsection{Simulation kinematics and feedback}
The simulated arm is represented by two independent arm variables, $r$ and $l$,
with linked joint coordinates such as
\[
q_{\mathrm{arm1}}=r,\qquad q_{\mathrm{arm2}}=l-r,\qquad
q_{\mathrm{endpoint}}=-l,\qquad q_{\mathrm{rod}}=l.
\]
The inverse calculation minimizes fingertip-centre error in the model's $x$--$z$
plane and rejects residuals above 0.002 m or solutions outside the saved joint and
coupling limits. Each commanded joint transition uses the quintic blend
$s(u)=10u^3-15u^4+6u^5$ to avoid discontinuous reference velocity.

Fresh bilateral gripper contact, retained-object height and placement conditions
gate later phases. Placement requires horizontal error below 23 mm, height error
below 8 mm and table contact. Home acceptance separately checks heading,
translation and arm error for a stable interval.

\subsection{Physical control workflow}
The final hardware script initializes the RoboMaster in AP connection mode and
commands the arm, gripper and chassis through the Python SDK. After each lift it
stops the chassis, clears old keyboard input and waits up to 30 s for a fresh
human confirmation that the object is retained. Only then is chassis rotation
allowed. Odd cycles turn $+32^\circ$ from A to B; even cycles turn $-32^\circ$
from B to A. After cycle 5 the remaining heading is restored before the arm moves
through HIGH to HOME.

The confirmation is a supervised safety checkpoint, not a force or vision
measurement. Cleanup pauses the gripper, commands zero wheel speed and closes the
SDK connection, but it is not described as a certified emergency-stop system.

\section{Simulation Development and Results}
\subsection{Scene, resources and execution}
The scene includes a $1.6\times1.2\times0.05$ m table, a 25 mm cube and fixed
points $A=(0.278,-0.001,0.0725)$ m and $B=(0.237,0.1453,0.0725)$ m. The cube's
simulation mass is 50 g; that setting is not a measurement of the real object.
The launch file starts Gazebo, the bridge, model spawning, state feedback and the
task controller. Bundled community model resources are attributed to
\href{https://github.com/jeguzzi/robomaster_ros}{jeguzzi/robomaster\_ros}, archived

\section{Problems, Solutions and Evidence Limits}
\subsection{Model resources and reference frames}
Early model problems included unresolved mesh paths and inconsistent end-effector
references. The final archive bundles the model dependency and provides
\code{prepare_paths.py} to construct a runtime copy with local mesh paths. The
kinematic calculation uses the forward ends of gripper link 4 rather than a rear
linkage. This separates a resource-resolution failure from a control failure.

\subsection{Unstable lift and false closure}
A gripper-close command is not evidence that an object is held. The final
simulation combines bilateral fresh contact, a small closure preload, a hold
interval and an object-height check before transfer. The physical script uses a
50 mm lift followed by a fresh human confirmation. Neither mechanism proves
gripping force, but each prevents a simple command-completion flag from being
treated as a successful grasp.

\subsection{Repeated-cycle state and heading restoration}
Earlier repeated sequences could drift or stop after one cycle. The final
simulation explicitly returns heading and home, records five placement checks,
five home checks and four refill acknowledgements, and ends after cycle 5 without
an extra refill. The physical script alternates directions and compensates the
remaining heading after cycle 5.

\subsection{Abnormal-case evidence}
The archived notes record eight passing offline tests across model and five-cycle
checks. An unreachable target such as $(10,10,0.6)$ is rejected by the kinematic
solver with a \code{ValueError}; joint and coupling limits are checked before
motion. Runtime stale-feedback and phase-timeout guards also exist in the
controller. These are useful safeguards, but no separate system-level fault-
injection run or individual safety-check record was supplied.

\subsection{Reproducibility notes}
The simulation targets ROS 2 Humble and Gazebo Fortress on the team's Jetson
Linux environment. The archived startup wrapper contains a workspace path that
must be adapted on another machine. The model dependency is bundled with source
attribution. Saved \code{results.json}, \code{events.jsonl} and
\code{trajectory.csv} support independent checking of the reported simulation
numbers without rerunning Gazebo.

\subsection{Conclusion}
The available evidence supports five successful simulation cycles with home
verification and five team-confirmed physical transfers without observed
abnormality. The project demonstrates a complete fixed-point sequence on the
available RoboMaster EP platform. Its strongest quantitative evidence is the
saved simulation record; the physical result is supported by the team-observed
outcome and a separate real-device video, not by instrumented precision data.
